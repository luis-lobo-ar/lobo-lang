try operand is prefix propagation: exactly two payload cases Ok(T),Err(E) are required in operand and enclosing output; error types match exactly, no conversion. Ok yields its payload; Err exits only the enclosing FUNCTION, including from loops or branches. Callers continue with the returned result. Direct prefix try is forbidden in requires/ensures. A PURE helper may use try in its own body and be called by a contract. The identifier try is legal for functions/variables. try(value) and try (value) are ordinary function calls, not propagation; they require a callable named try. Runtime faults are not caught. Postconditions run on propagated errors too. No implicit resource cleanup is added.
